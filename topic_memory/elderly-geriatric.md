# Topic Memory: Elderly & Geriatric
_Last updated: 2026-11-02 · source_id: elderly-geriatric · covers studies through 2026-11-01_

## Established findings
- **virtual-remote-delivery-older-adults** — Virtually delivered palliative, rehabilitation and lifestyle programs for older adults are implementable, cost-effective and produce gains comparable to or better than usual care, though rural uptake stays low. (PMIDs 41166741, 42713766, 42749466, 42782261, 42772608, 42910563)
- **goals-of-care-conversations-late** — Documented end-of-life preferences remain rare, are triggered mainly by hospitalization, arrive close to death, and translate poorly into the care patients and families expect. (PMIDs 42250861, 42337874, 42790554, 42766491, 42781834, 42165567)
- **simple-geriatric-screens-predict-outcomes** — Low-burden measures — claims-based frailty indices, grip strength, balance and step counts, six-minute walk distance, bedside shock index with consciousness, and routine blood indices — predict mortality and functional decline. (PMIDs 41379576, 42639683, 42806885, 42888018, 42900878, 42914502, 42906648)
- **palliative-access-barriers** — Specialty palliative care reaches rural, minority, immigrant, disabled and low-income patients least, and services rarely accommodate the language, religious practice or cultural framing these patients rely on. (PMIDs 42141843, 42658237, 42773632, 42386162, 42860763, 42929285, 42895139, 42895138)
- **family-caregiver-needs-palliative** — Family caregivers absorb invisible coordination and bedside symptom work, face structural barriers to home end-of-life support, and report depression, anxiety and unmet needs of their own. (PMIDs 42685648, 42693513, 42771517, 42888399, 42906509, 42899455, 42902770)
- **dignity-relational-end-of-life** — End-of-life dignity and treatment choices hinge on relational factors — clinician word choice, listening and cultural understanding — which burnout and language mismatch erode. (PMIDs 42861474, 42842360, 42910992)
- **ai-in-palliative-care** — Large language models and conversational AI approach clinician accuracy on palliative knowledge, geriatric vulnerability screening and qualitative analysis, but validation is thin and patients trust AI-assisted clinicians less. (PMIDs 42231120, 40598879, 42697288, 42599269, 42950609, 42965520)

## Emerging threads
- **rehab-outcome-measures-miss-function** — Rehabilitation assessments capture impairment, strength or prevalence rather than everyday function, so participation-focused measures reveal disability and activity limitation that standard measures miss. (PMIDs 42788421, 40519080, 42838842, 42924528; first seen 2026-09-29)
- **acp-uptake-interventions** — Simple prompts — paper tools, EHR nudges, nurse-led conversations and a yes/no admission screening question — increase advance care planning documentation and bring palliative consultation earlier. (PMIDs 42681826, 42778351, 42806888; first seen 2026-09-03)
- **rheumatic-remission-de-escalation** — Remission now arrives faster but is less durable, with relapse rising over decades, residual low disease activity predicting flare and prolonged glucocorticoids adding harm without benefit. (PMIDs 42767416, 42765471, 42778334, 42839717, 42919621; first seen 2026-09-23)
- **low-value-care-older-adults** — Older adults frequently receive guideline-discordant care, from imaging instead of physical therapy for new back pain to near-universal hospice deprescribing without symptom-outcome evidence. (PMIDs 42852584, 42848201, 42897830; first seen 2026-10-15)
- **eol-delirium-recognition-gap** — Delirium in dying and hospitalized older patients is often unnamed by staff, left to families at home, and the sedatives used show no measurable calming effect. (PMIDs 42902770, 42910992, 42924868; first seen 2026-10-26)
- **wish-to-hasten-death-palliative** — A wish to hasten death is expressed by about a third of advanced cancer patients, driven more by demoralization than pain, and deeply distresses family who hear it. (PMIDs 42918739, 42896718; first seen 2026-10-26)
- **discharge-decisions-subjective-older-adults** — Discharge planning for older inpatients follows clinician intuition, patient self-appraisal and socioeconomic status more than structured readiness assessment, misstating readiness and narrowing destination. (PMIDs 42911136, 42950358; first seen 2026-10-28)

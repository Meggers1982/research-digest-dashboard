# Topic Memory: Children's & Pediatric Health
_Last updated: 2026-10-23 · source_id: pediatric-health · covers studies through 2026-10-18_

## Established findings
- **single-center-biomarkers-pediatric-disease** — Small single-center pediatric studies keep proposing biomarkers, imaging indices, bedside devices and monitoring metrics to detect pain, organ damage or deterioration, with modest discrimination and no external validation. (PMIDs 42684427, 42786197, 42867571, 42853414, 42863668)
- **guideline-discordant-pediatric-workup** — Delivered pediatric care diverges widely from recommended standards in investigation, imaging, death review, child protection referral, vaccination coverage and time to diagnosis. (PMIDs 42135984, 42692799, 42820507, 42853409, 42855362)

## Emerging threads
- **adult-evidence-extrapolated-to-children** — Findings promoted as relevant to youth — nutrition, exercise, measurement scales, neurocognition, illness experience — are tested almost entirely in adult samples with no pediatric subgroup. (PMIDs 42841447, 42862435, 42837995, 42844656; first seen 2026-09-24)
- **caregiver-burden-chronic-complex-children** — Parents of children with chronic or medically complex conditions report high fear of progression, anxiety exceeding their children's own, traumatic treatment responsibility and unreported home safety workarounds. (PMIDs 42858961, 42842426, 42864277, 42875029; first seen 2026-10-12)
- **adolescent-phone-use-depression-parenting** — In Chinese school self-report samples, depressive symptoms and harsh parenting are linked to problematic smartphone use and phone-mediated social withdrawal, with cognitive flexibility a candidate bridge. (PMIDs 42837874, 42861665; first seen 2026-10-12)
- **pediatric-screening-tools-single-sample-validation** — New pediatric and caregiver assessment instruments report strong psychometrics from single narrow samples, and independent reexamination of established tools exposes measurement flaws. (PMIDs 42701327, 42788798, 42804916, 42841447; first seen 2026-09-09)
- **early-life-nutrition-long-term-outcomes** — Exposures in pregnancy and the first 1,000 days — congenital infection, maternal physiology, feeding and the gut microbiome — track with children's later neurodevelopment, regulation and body composition. (PMIDs 42766296, 42706995, 42066677, 42844698; first seen 2026-09-02)
- **early-markers-predict-later-development** — Early neural and behavioral measures forecast later outcomes, including infant EEG predicting socioemotional problems and kindergarten attention predicting math skills. (PMIDs 42671816, 42107140, 40681146; first seen 2026-09-05)
- **low-yield-routine-testing** — Routine escalation adds little: neuroimaging and EEG rarely explained first-episode pediatric psychosis, empiric MRSA coverage lengthened stays, and routine cardiac screening in X-linked ichthyosis found normal hearts. (PMIDs 42645338, 42702367, 42855658; first seen 2026-09-02)
- **new-pediatric-therapies-monitoring-gap** — Pediatric services lack surveillance for newer drugs and therapies, from GLP-1 receptor agonists and AAV gene therapy to neuropsychiatric reactions to second-generation antihistamines. (PMIDs 42692883, 42697506, 42848058; first seen 2026-09-05)
- **implementation-barriers-child-services** — Program and provider reports place the bottleneck in implementation: resisted trauma-informed care, strained newborn screening, unprepared epilepsy transition, and stigma-driven treatment dropout among adolescents with HIV. (PMIDs 42641570, 42674592, 42702196, 42790888, 42832299; first seen 2026-09-01)
- **individual-factors-adolescent-eating-disorders** — Beyond shared genetics, individual and cultural factors distinguish adolescents who develop disordered eating, with traditional food practices appearing protective and generic exercise promotion possibly harmful. (PMIDs 42429700, 42683660, 42760826; first seen 2026-09-08)
- **feeding-tolerance-vulnerable-children** — Feeding management in fragile pediatric groups is being revisited: early enteral feeding was safe in growth-restricted preterm infants, and feeding difficulties and muscle loss go unrecognized in children on specialized nutrition. (PMIDs 42789183, 42785553, 42863668; first seen 2026-09-28)
